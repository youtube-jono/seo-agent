## What counts as a plumbing emergency in Toronto?

Not everything is a 2 a.m. phone call. Here's the honest line between "call now" and "call Monday":

- **Call now:** burst or frozen pipe, sewage backup, no water at all, a leak you can't stop, water near electrical, no heat in January (because Toronto winters do not negotiate).
- **Can probably wait:** a single slow drain, a drippy faucet, a running toilet that's annoying but contained.

If it's the first list, you want a **24 hour plumber in Toronto** on the phone. If it's the second, you can book normal-hours service and keep roughly half your money.

## The 60-second emergency checklist

1. **Shut off the water** (main valve, or the local valve under the fixture).
2. **Kill the power** to any area where water is meeting electrical. Water and electricity are not friends.
3. **Open a downstairs faucet** to drain the remaining water away from the leak.
4. **Move the valuables**, grab towels, and resist the urge to "just look at it" with a wrench.
5. **Call an after-hours plumber** and describe what's happening - they'll tell you what to do until they arrive.

## Why Toronto homes flood at the worst possible time

It's not bad luck, it's physics with a grudge. Older Toronto neighbourhoods (hello, anything pre-1960) often have aging galvanized or clay pipes that pick the coldest night of the year to retire dramatically. Add freeze-thaw cycles, heavy spring storms overwhelming the drains, and basement backups, and you get a city that keeps emergency plumbers very, very employed.

The fix isn't superstition - it's a **backwater valve**, pipe insulation, and the occasional inspection. Boring? Yes. Cheaper than a flooded basement at midnight? Also yes.

## How to pick an emergency plumber without getting fleeced

When you're stressed and dripping, you'll say yes to almost anything. Don't. A 20-second gut check:

- **Licensed and insured** - so "oops" is their problem, not your homeowner's policy.
- **Upfront / flat-rate pricing** - the quote you're told is the price you pay, no mystery line items.
- **Actually 24/7** - not "24/7" that goes to voicemail until Tuesday.
- **Real, recent reviews** - five stars from this year, not a dusty testimonial from 2019.
- **A warranty** on the work - round two should not cost you twice.

## What to do while you wait for the plumber

Keep the water off. Keep towels moving. Take photos of the damage for insurance, because your future self filing a claim will thank you.

Don't pour chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up line - it just turns a clog into a hazardous clog. And don't YouTube your way into the main line at 2 a.m. "I watched a video once" is how a $300 visit becomes a $3,000 one.
