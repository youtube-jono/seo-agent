## The numbers to anchor the post

**Standard hourly rate** · $45 to $200 an hour. Near-universal across sources.

**National average rate** · about $90 an hour, with a second camp at $100 to $110. HomeAdvisor, Fixr and Modernize say $90. This Old House and Plumbing Price Guide say $100 to $110.

**Residential everyday band** · $80 to $130 an hour, per Angi, HomeGuide and HousecallPro.

**Typical job total** · about $340, ranging $99 to $980. Angi and HomeAdvisor say $339, Bob Vila $331, Thumbtack $331 for a drain.

**Service or call-out fee** · $50 to $150, sometimes including the first hour. This is the dominant range.

**Emergency multiplier** · 1.5 to 3 times the standard rate, near-universal.

**Emergency hourly rate** · $150 to $300 an hour in most sources.

**Rate by licence tier** · apprentice about $45 to $90 · journeyman about $70 to $120 · master about $90 to $200 and up.

### Lead the article with the wage-versus-rate gap

A plumber's *wage*, meaning what the worker earns, is not the same as the *rate*, meaning what you pay. The US plumber median wage is $30.27 an hour according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, but homeowners pay about $90 an hour. The 3x gap is overhead, insurance, parts, truck, licence and training.

This is the single thing Reddit and Quora users are most confused and angry about, and the biggest trust-building opportunity in the post.

### The seven things videos say that articles won't

1. **Flat rate is a margin strategy, not just transparency.** Coaches teach plumbers to switch to flat rate because it lifts the average ticket 20 to 30%. Articles never admit this.
2. **Who eats the overrun is the real flat-versus-hourly difference.** Flat rate transfers the risk of a job running long to the plumber, so the number is padded for it.
3. **Showing up is paid labour with a stopwatch running.** "A 30-minute roundtrip with 15 minutes on site is 45 paid minutes before any wrench turns", plus the cost of not being at another job.
4. **The service fee filters out free-estimate tire-kickers**, and it is often waived when you book.
5. **The auto-repair comparison**, from Wakefield: people accept a mechanic's diagnostic fee but balk at a plumber's. You are paying for diagnosis accuracy, and a misdiagnosis costs more than the fee.
6. **Overhead spoken out loud** - a roughly $23,000 plumbing degree, 5,000 psi jetter trucks, and legally required insurance "because they handle thousands of gallons of water".
7. **Scarcity is priced in.** Good plumbers are booked solid, so fees ration demand.

### The three most quotable lines

- "You don't pay the plumber for banging on the pipe. You pay him for knowing where to bang."
- "A 30-minute roundtrip with 15 minutes on site is 45 paid minutes before any wrench turns."
- "Behind every quality plumbing company is an enormous amount of overhead that most people never see."

## Technical facts competitors get wrong: 8

**What the licence tiers actually require**
Apprentice works supervised, 3 to 5 years of training, no exam. Journeyman needs roughly 4,000 to 8,000 hours plus a code exam, and works unsupervised. Master adds about 2 more years plus a management and design exam, and can pull permits and run a business.
https://explorethetrades.org/blog/plumbers-apprenticeship-journeyman-and-master-plumbers-understanding-the-difference/

**How much it varies by state**
In Texas a journeyman needs 8,000 hours, a 48-hour course, and a $40 exam. Elsewhere: Maryland 7,500 hours, Maine 8,000, Michigan 4,000.
https://tsbpe.texas.gov/license-types/journeyman/

**Rate by tier, which most posts flatten**
Apprentice about $45 to $90, journeyman about $70 to $120, master about $90 to $200 and up. From Bob Vila, This Old House and Fixr.

**Trip fee, diagnostic fee and service-call fee are three different things**
A trip or dispatch fee is about $25 to $75 and covers travel and fuel. A diagnostic fee is about $50 to $125 and covers inspecting and identifying the problem. A service call is $75 to $300 and bundles travel plus the first hour of diagnosis. ServiceTitan data across a million-plus jobs shows diagnostic fees cluster at $0, then $89 to $99 which is most common, then $125 to $175, then $300 and up. They are often credited toward the bill if you approve the work, so asking upfront saves $50 to $150.
https://www.servicetitan.com/field-service-management/perfect-diagnostic-fee

**Flat rate against time and materials**
Flat rate is one fixed total agreed before work starts, which buys cost certainty. Time and materials bills hours plus parts, and is used when the scope is unpredictable, such as hidden leaks or old homes. A hybrid quotes flat for the first hour or two, then hourly.
https://www.rotorooter.com/blog/plumbing/flat-rate-pricing-vs-hourly-pricing/

**PEX against copper, separating material from labour**
Copper material $2 to $8 a foot against PEX $0.40 to $2. Copper labour $4 to $10 a foot, because every joint is soldered, against PEX at $1.50 to $3. A whole-home repipe on 2,000 square feet is $8,000 to $12,000 in copper against $4,000 to $7,000 in PEX. Copper prices swing with commodities, PEX is stable.
https://www.angi.com/articles/should-i-install-pex-or-copper-piping.htm

**Why permits cost what they do**
The fee pays for plan review and inspections. $50 to $250 for simple residential, $200 to $500 and up for major or sewer work. Rough-in and final inspections are both required.
https://www.permitflow.com/blog/plumbing-permit

**"Get three quotes" with an actual threshold**
On jobs over $500, three or more quotes reveal the market rate. An unrealistically low bid is a red flag.
https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/plumbing/hire-a-plumber/
