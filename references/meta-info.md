# Write titles and meta descriptions that get clicked

The swipe set for title tags and meta descriptions, plus the judgment calls a checklist cannot make. Updated Wednesday 13 August 2026.
Next: write 2 to 3 title and meta variants per page, then run the 3 gut checks and pick one.

**How this file fits with the checklist.** [on-page-seo.md](on-page-seo.md) grades titles and descriptions on the mechanical rules - length, keyword placement, uniqueness. Two titles can both pass all 8 of those checks and only one gets clicked. This file is the part that closes that gap.

**Why it is worth the effort.** At the same ranking position, a stronger title or description can lift clicks 20 to 40 percent. Rewriting descriptions on high-impression pages can roughly double click-through rate. You do not have to rank higher to get more traffic, you have to get chosen at the moment of decision.

## Use these lifts as tie-breakers

Stacking all of them produces clickbait, which Google punishes. See "What kills your click-through rate" below.

**Brackets or parentheses modifier, up 38 percent**
Things like `[2026 Update]` or `(With Examples)`. HubSpot study of 3.3 million titles.

**Numbers written as numerals, up 36 percent**
Use `7`, not `seven`. "7 Strategies" also beats "Several Strategies" by about 23 percent. Sources: seoscore and Straight North.

**Current year in the title, up 27 percent**
For example `2026`. Source: seoscore.

**Power words, up 14 percent**
Source: seoscore.

**Question-format title, up 14 percent**
Source: seoscore.

**Emotional title instead of a flat one, up 2.5 percent**
Source: title-tag click-through studies.

**Moving the keyword to the FRONT of the meta description, up 15 to 25 percent**
Source: SearchScale masterclass.

## Title tag rules that never move

- **50-60 characters** - longer truncates with "...", often cutting the brand or the year off the end
- **Primary keyword in the first one or two words** - earns the click AND signals topical relevance, which now also influences whether AI Overviews cite you
- **Front-load the message** - if it truncates, the part that matters must survive
- **Title and H1 on the same topic** - the single strongest defense against Google rewriting your title for you
- **Unique per page** - duplicate titles across service and location pages dilute everything

## Pick a title formula

Each one is fill-in-the-brackets. Pick the one that fits the page's intent.

1. **Number + keyword + promise** - `10 [Keyword] Mistakes That Kill Your Rankings (And How to Fix Them)`
2. **Keyword + colon + benefit** - `[Keyword]: How to [Outcome] in 2026`
3. **How-to + outcome** - `How to [Achieve Outcome] Without [Pain]`
4. **Keyword + year + bracket modifier** - `[Keyword]: The Complete Guide [2026 Update]`
5. **Question, the searcher's exact question** - `How Much Does [Thing] Actually Cost? (Real Quotes)`
6. **Real-proof angle, Jono's lane** - `[Outcome]: What [Real Number] of [Real Work] Taught Me`

**The taste call, worked through.** "Water Heater Cost - 2026 Guide" against "How Much Is a New Water Heater? Real Quotes." Both are legal. The second reads as the searcher's literal question plus a concrete payoff. Phrase it the way the human typed it, then promise something specific.

## Power words that pull

Five categories that test well. One or two per title, never a pile-up.

- **Urgency** - Now, Today, Fast, Instantly
- **Specificity** - Exact, Complete, Step-by-Step, Proven
- **Value** - Free, Boost, Double, Save
- **Authority** - Expert, Masterclass, Guide, Science-Backed
- **Year** - 2026, This Year, Updated

## Meta description rules

- **Length 140-160 characters** is the safe zone, but put the most important message in the **first 120 characters** - mobile truncates early
- **Primary keyword once, near the front** - it gets bolded in the result and draws the eye; front-loading it lifts click-through rate 15 to 25 percent
- **Write for the human, not the bot** - Google rewrites the description about 62 percent of the time, so a keyword-stuffed one just gets thrown out and auto-generated. Keyword relevance and length are the two rewrite triggers you can actually control
- **One clear call to action** - turns a passive blurb into an invitation
- **Unique per page** - same rule as titles

## The meta description formula

**Action verb + primary keyword + specific benefit or contents + call to action**

**Template:** `[Verb] [keyword] - [what's inside / specific deliverable], [the benefit]. [CTA].`

**Example:** `See what a new water heater really costs - real 2026 quotes, install fees, and the cheapest brand that lasts. Get your free estimate.`

## Call-to-action phrases that pull

- Learn more
- Get started today
- Discover how
- See the real numbers
- Call today
- Book a free consultation
- Get your free estimate
- Read the full guide

## What kills your click-through rate

**Clickbait that misleads.** Gets the click, then a bounce, and Google deprioritizes you for it. The title must match what is on the page.

**Keyword stuffing.** Reads spammy, lowers clicks, and triggers a Google rewrite.

**Truncation.** Over 60 characters on the title, or over about 155 characters on the description, cuts off the part you cared about.

**Duplicate titles or descriptions.** Common on location and product pages. Dilutes relevance signals.

**Burying the keyword at the end.** Least visual and least algorithmic weight.

## Read this before you obsess over click-through rate

- About **65 percent of Google searches now end with zero clicks**, and on queries that trigger an AI Overview, organic click-through rate has dropped as much as 61 percent
- The clicks that survive **convert about 23 percent better**, and brands cited inside AI Overviews get about 120 percent more organic clicks per impression
- AI systems read your **content and structure**, largely independent of your title tag. The title still earns the human click on traditional results, while the answer-first blocks, tables, and entities in [on-page-seo.md](on-page-seo.md) section 12 earn the AI citation
- **Takeaway:** keep writing high-click titles for the traditional click, but do not expect titles alone to win the AI surface. Citation is earned in the body, not the head tag

## The 3 gut checks - run on every chosen variant

The checklist passed it. Now you pass it. AI cannot make these three calls.

1. **Does the title earn the click?** Both variants are legal. Only one gets chosen. Say it out loud and ask which you would click in a list of 10 blue links.
2. **Does it match intent?** The keyword can be everywhere and the real answer still missing. Read the title and description as the person who typed the search. Did you promise what they actually wanted?
3. **Would a human read it?** The read-it-out-loud test. If you stumble or cringe, a reader bounces. Fix the voice before it ships.

---

## The workflow

1. Generator writes **2 to 3 title and meta variants** using the formulas above
2. Each variant must pass [on-page-seo.md](on-page-seo.md) section 1, the mechanical gate
3. Human runs the 3 gut checks and picks the winner
4. **The A/B opportunity later:** in Google Search Console, filter for pages at positions 4 to 8 with high impressions but low click-through rate. Google already ranks them, humans just are not choosing them. Those are your highest-value rewrite targets. Rewrite the title to answer the query directly and add specificity, ship it, and watch click-through rate over 2 to 4 weeks
