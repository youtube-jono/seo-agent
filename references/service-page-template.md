# Service Page Template - THE locked money-page structure

Every service and city page `/service-page` writes uses THIS skeleton. Built once from [cro-cheatsheet.md](../references/cro-cheatsheet.md) + [on-page-seo.md](../references/on-page-seo.md) + [geo.md](../references/geo.md) - the structure never changes, only the proof and the service. Improve THIS file, never deviate per-page.

The blog template entertains to keep readers; this one **proves to convert buyers**. Humour dial: charm, not comedy - one or two grins, the proof does the talking.

---

## The skeleton (section order is fixed)

### 1. Title + meta
Message match is law: the title says exactly what the searcher typed. 2-3 variants per [meta-info.md](../references/meta-info.md), transactional framing ("Emergency Drain Cleaning Toronto - Same-Day Service"), primary keyword front-loaded.

### 2. Hero - the 5-second sale
- **H1** = the primary keyword phrased the way they searched it
- **ONE call to action**, visible without scrolling: click-to-call button (mobile) / short form (desktop). No competing choices.
- **The proof line** directly under the H1: stars + exact review count + license/insured + years ("4.9★ from 212 reviews · Licensed & insured · Since 2011") - every item from proof-inventory
- The quick answer (40-60 words): what we do, where, how fast - AI-liftable, kept straight

### 3. The proof stack (first scroll)
3-4 proof blocks, each one real: a tagged review quote (speed/price/quality/trust), a real job photo with caption, the guarantee stated plainly, a named credential. **CTA repeats after this stack.**

### 4. What's included
The service, concretely: what happens when they call, what's covered, timeline. A **table** where the topic supports it (pricing tiers, options, timelines - the GEO citation lever AND the buyer's comparison need). Real prices from proof-inventory where the business publishes them.

### 5. Jobs we've done (the uncopyable section)
2-3 real jobs from proof-inventory: the situation, what we did, the number that resulted. City pages: jobs IN that city - this is the anti-clone rule made concrete. First-person, specific.

### 6. FAQ - the objection killers
4-6 questions, the real ones: cost, timing, guarantee, "do you serve [area]", the trade-specific worry. Answered honestly in 2-3 tight sentences, phrased as searched. Kept straight (AI lifts these).

### 7. Service area block
Cities/neighborhoods served, stated in plain text (the model must READ where you work). City pages: this narrows to the city's neighborhoods. Out-of-area visitors self-select out - that's a feature.

### 8. Final CTA + the form
The close: guarantee restated + the form (3-4 fields max, button text = the outcome: "Get My Free Quote") or the call button. Thank-you page sets the callback expectation and fires the conversion tag.

### Schema (invisible layer, every page)
Service + LocalBusiness (NAP matching GBP exactly) + Organization, validating clean.

### Internal links
Up to the /services/ index, across to sibling city pages of the same service, and every related blog post links DOWN to this page (money pages end up the most-linked pages on the site).

## ⛔ Progressive disclosure - collapse the depth, never the sale

**A money page is sales copy, not a document. Less is more - hide everything you can.**

**⛔ The default is COLLAPSED. Visible is the exception and it has to be argued for.** Do not ask "should this be hidden" - ask "does this page fail to sell without this on screen". If the answer is no, it goes in an accordion. A money page that reads like a thorough article is a money page that loses to a shorter one.

**The whole visible page should read in under 30 seconds on a phone.** Everything written for SEO coverage, AI citation and the one-in-ten researcher goes behind a click. The ready buyer never scrolls through it, the thorough buyer opens it, and Google gets the full text either way.

**This costs nothing in rankings.** Under mobile-first indexing, content inside accordions and tabs is indexed and weighted the same as visible content. The old "never hide text" advice is stale. **Write the depth in full - then collapse nearly all of it.**

**The ONLY things that stay visible.** This list is complete. Anything not on it collapses:

- The H1 and the hero promise
- The offer, the price and the guarantee
- **One** proof line - the strongest single number or review, not the stack
- Every CTA and the form
- One plain-text line naming the service area

**Everything else collapses**, including things that feel too important to hide - the full proof stack, what's included, the process, jobs done, spec tables, materials, timelines, the service-area detail, and **every FAQ answer** (questions visible, answers closed - the question stack alone should read as the buyer's real worries).

**How to write a collapsed section:** the visible label carries the whole meaning on its own. "Our process" is a wasted line; "What happens in the first 48 hours" is a reason to click. **Assume nobody opens it.** If a fact decides the sale, it does not go inside - it goes on the short list above or it gets cut.

**One accordion open by default**, the dominant objection, so the pattern is visibly a pattern and skimmers know there is more.

**Never collapse:** the answer-first paragraph under an H2 that has to win a snippet, the guarantee, or anything the CRO cheatsheet requires next to the form.

**The test:** screenshot the page with every accordion shut. If that screenshot does not sell on its own, the page is wrong - and the fix is cutting or promoting one line, never opening a section back up.

## Rules

- **Sections 1-8 in this order, every page.** Expand, never delete or reorder. No real jobs for section 5 yet = the best true material available, honestly framed - never invented.
- **Collapse the depth, keep the sale visible.** Per the progressive-disclosure rule above - the page reads in 30 seconds, the detail is one click away, and nothing conversion-critical is ever behind a click.
- **Proof gate: 5+ proof touches minimum** per page, every one traceable to proof-inventory. Count them before registering.
- **City pages are never clones.** Real local jobs, local neighborhoods, city-specific FAQ answers - or flag and skip the city rather than ship a find-and-replace page.
- **The CRO walkthrough still runs** after the draft: all 7 cheatsheet items, quoted where satisfied, honest where not.
- **Improve the template, not the instance.** Better pattern found? Update THIS file with a changelog line.
